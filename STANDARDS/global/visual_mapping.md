# Semantic Visual Mapping Standard

## Trigger

Apply this standard whenever an instructional artifact uses visual attributes to connect an explanation with code, a diagram component, a formula element, a state, a category, or another visual region.

## Map meaning before styling

Before assigning colors or other visual treatments:

1. identify every explanatory category;
2. identify the exact token, object, formula element, or region corresponding to each category;
3. assign one stable semantic treatment to each relationship; and
4. keep unrelated content visually neutral enough that it does not compete with the instructional mapping.

## Background-paired semantic colors

When related elements appear on backgrounds with materially different luminance:

- use the same semantic color family;
- use a higher-luminance variant on a dark background;
- use a lower-luminance variant on a light background;
- preserve a recognizable relationship between the variants; and
- verify contrast separately against each immediate background.

Exact hexadecimal identity is not required. Semantic correspondence and local readability take priority.

## Non-color reinforcement

Do not rely on color alone. Reinforce important mappings with at least one additional cue, such as:

- an explicit label;
- bold type;
- an outline;
- a symbol;
- matching position;
- a connector;
- a shape; or
- spatial grouping.

## Instructional highlighting

Instructional meaning takes priority over generic syntax highlighting. When ordinary syntax colors conflict with the concept being taught, use the instructional mapping and leave unrelated syntax neutral.

Avoid assigning too many categories at once. If the mapping becomes difficult to distinguish, simplify the explanation or split it across stages.

## Final review

Inspect the final rendered artifact at likely delivery scale. Confirm that:

- every descriptor points to the intended target;
- the same semantic family is used consistently;
- each local color has sufficient contrast;
- the mapping remains understandable without color; and
- projection, export, or platform rendering did not weaken the treatment.
